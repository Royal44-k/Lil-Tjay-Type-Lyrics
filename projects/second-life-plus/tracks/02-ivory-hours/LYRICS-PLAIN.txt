Back from the service, I sit at your keys,
Cup-ring moon on the lid by the lamp.
I could sell it for less than the movers would need,
But I can't lift the phone with that number in hand.
You taught me the left hand and hated my timing,
Tapped on the wood till I swallowed the tune.
I learned to play softly to keep you from sighing;
Now I hit that wrong note, let it stay in the room.

We carry a different old man in our heads.
He fixed every hinge, never said he was sorry;
I'm sorting the screws from the things left unsaid.

Found a small tape with a date on the label,
You counting me in, then forgetting the count.
I laugh at the part where you knock on the table;
You laugh right behind me. I hadn't heard that sound.
For years I had filed you in one little folder:
The voice in the hall when the bottle got low.
But this doesn't cancel the nights I got older
Before I was ready, or make them less so.
I keep both the takes when I cut up the sample,
The soft little hum and the crack in the chair.
Forgiveness is still not a word I can manage;
Tonight I just leave a small gap in the snare.

I don't have to make you good
To miss you when the house gets still.
