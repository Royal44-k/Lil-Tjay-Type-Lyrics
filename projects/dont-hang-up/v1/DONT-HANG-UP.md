# DON'T HANG UP

Original English melodic trap composition · draft 1 · 2026-09-17

148 BPM / half-time feel 74 · C minor · 4/4

Concept: an emerging musician can fill a room with listeners but struggles to let one person hear him. A late-night phone call begins as a request for company and becomes a promise to offer it back. The narrator is fictional.

Status: full lyric draft, original Hook and sample-mother toplines, MIDI and recording/production brief. No human vocal session or finished master has been produced. The sample text and melody below are newly written; there is no borrowed artist recording.

Had a whole speech lined up in my head.

You heard the part I hadn't said.

## Delivery notes

- Hook: favor chest/head blend and clear stressed vowels. Let “up” hang; don't belt every word. The title uses a repeated six-syllable unit, with a small change in pitch on its second appearance.
- Verse 1: clipped, conversational first four bars; softer articulation on the message to Mom. The quoted complaint should sound remembered, not delivered as a punchline. Let “You eaten yet?” be small and ordinary.
- Verse 2: move from possession to habit to reciprocal care. Dense lines can use passing sixteenth notes or brief triplet groups. Do not give every word equal weight. Take breaths at the semicolons and use the endings of bars 4, 8 and 12 as breath space.
- Final Hook: “I” can be a short pickup or a very short first syllable. Keep the title's three strong accents on WON'T / HANG / UP. “I won't” replaces one weak onset plus the original DON'T; the final-hook MIDI supports this variant.
- The title is an invitation to stay present, not an assertion that another person must solve the narrator's life.
