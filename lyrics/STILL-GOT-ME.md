# STILL GOT ME

Original English-language American melodic trap song. Written 2026-09-11.

Theme: ambition, roots, and keeping one's identity through a come-up. A fictional emerging rapper wants financial security for his family and recognition for his music. Pride, status anxiety, loyalty, and gratitude complicate that pursuit.

## Production and delivery

- Genre: American hip-hop / melodic trap. English lyrics composed directly for English stress and phrasing.
- DAW tempo: **144 BPM**, half-time feel **72 BPM**, **F-sharp minor**.
- Harmony: **Dmaj7 / Aadd9 / F#m9 / Esus2**. Each chord lasts two bars at 144 BPM.
- Arrangement: muted plucked motif, sparse electric keys, saturated sub/808, syncopated kick, snare on beat three at 144 BPM, sixteenth-note subdivisions and short hat rolls. Keep space around the voice.
- Vocal direction: intimate mid/high-register sung rap, audible pitch correction if desired, short phrase-end slides, restrained doubles. Rap the more detailed lines; let stressed rhyme words stretch. Use one's own voice rather than a specific artist's vocal identity.
- Suggested full form, counted at 144 BPM: Intro 4 / Hook 16 / Verse 1 32 / Hook 16 / Verse 2 32 / Bridge 8 / Final Hook 16 / Outro 4. Total 128 bars, approximately **3:33** before reverb tail.
- Each written verse line spans about two fast-grid bars. Short lines have deliberate rests; long lines compress unstressed syllables into passing subdivisions. Do not flatten every word into an equal-length note.
- Verse 1, lines 1–4: matter-of-fact family memory. Lines 5–8: slightly sharper, self-conscious delivery. Lines 9–12: half-smile, then pause after "damn." Lines 13–16: drop density and bring the melody forward.
- Verse 2, lines 1–4: admit responsibility instead of blaming everyone else. Lines 5–8: retain ambition; stress "money matters" and "freedom." Lines 9–12: soften, with room for a breath after "finished." Lines 13–16: build back toward the final hook.

## Hook writing

The central phrase uses a six-syllable question-and-answer motif: **STILL got ME / STILL got ME**. "Still" and "me" carry the weight; "got" is a short connector. The first group descends; the second starts lower and turns upward.

The held words share the /iː/ vowel: **me / underneath / deep / beat**. In "underneath," the sustained emphasis goes on **neath**, preserving natural English stress. "Wants a piece, wants a piece" creates an internal repetition before returning to the title. The sixth line rises toward "high" and settles downward toward "deep."

The verses use vowel families and slant rhyme: **beside it / cryin' / inside**, **time / life / fine / eye**, **flames / page / race / changed**, **mother / another / hunger / summer**, **skin / again / in / win**. Some are near rhymes; delivery should preserve natural pronunciation. Internal vowel echoes such as **first check / month's rent** and **speech / sentence** can guide accents without forcing speech into a rigid grid.

## Meaning notes

- The chain is an attempt to make private worth publicly visible; the narrator admits its financial cost without pretending material comfort is unimportant.
- The friend's deal provokes both affection and envy. The narrator recognizes that another person's progress did not actually subtract from his own life.
- "A halo out of hunger" refuses to treat deprivation as automatically noble. The song still wants money and safety.
- The brother's phone call and the final spoken line show that "still got me" leaves room for support and intimacy.
- The bridge admits the intensity of ambition; the song ends with an ongoing choice rather than a claim that every insecurity has vanished.
